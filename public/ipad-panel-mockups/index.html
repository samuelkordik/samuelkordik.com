Klipper Control on an iPad

A retired iPad mini 2 becomes the dedicated touch panel for a Klipper 3D printer. One USB cable to the print host keeps it fast and dependable when the garage Wi-Fi isn’t, and every screen is designed to be read from across the room, with the controls you reach for most never more than a tap or two away.

The print screen

Your whole print, at a glance

Progress and time left are set in huge numerals with a segmented meter, so one look from 3 m away tells you the state, the finish time, and whether anything is wrong.

  • First-layer Z offset, front and centre. While layer 1 lays down, the print screen puts Z offset up top with Lower and Raise buttons, in 0.005 to 0.05 mm steps and shown to three decimals. From layer 2 it tucks into Adjust. Changes apply instantly, and when the print ends a Save Z button makes the good first layer stick.
  • Speed, flow and part fan, locked by default. Sliders are right there but behind a lock, so a stray touch can’t change a running print.
  • Live charts and stats. Nozzle and bed temperature over the last 30 minutes, plus layer, Z height, filament used, speed and flow.
  • Obico watching. The AI failure meter shows how worried it is, and it can pause the print for you.
  • Pause, Cancel, Adjust, Configure. And the red Emergency stop never leaves the bottom corner.

Beyond the print

Everything else, just as easy

Prep, calibrate and maintain without leaving the panel.

Move

Jog, home and set Z offset

Big jog arrows in steps from 0.1 to 100 mm, with homed axes in orange and un-homed ones in yellow, so you always know the state. Z arrows stay disabled until Z is homed. Retract or extrude, cut motors, and set and save Z offset without leaving the screen.

Bed mesh

See how flat your bed really is

An 8×8 heat map runs from blue (low) to orange (high), with the height in every cell. A summary gives range, highest, lowest and mean, and says in plain language where the bed sags. Switch to a 3D view, keep named profiles, or recalibrate in about three minutes.

Filament dryer

Dry filament on the heated bed

Pick a preset (PLA, PETG or ABS) or set your own time and temperature. A large countdown and segmented meter show how long is left and when it will finish, and the panel blocks printing while the dryer is using the bed.
